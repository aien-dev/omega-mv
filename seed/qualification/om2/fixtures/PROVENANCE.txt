1. SHA-256 constants: sha256-k.dec (K[0..63]) and sha256-iv.dec (H(0)[0..7]), decimal, one per line
   Derivation (exact integers, no floating point): K[i] = icbrt(p_i * 2^96) mod 2^32 and
   H[i] = isqrt(p_i * 2^64) mod 2^32. Here p_i is the i-th prime, found by trial division inside bc,
   and icbrt/isqrt are floor roots by integer Newton iteration. This equals the first 32 bits of the
   fractional parts of the cube and square roots, because floor(r * 2^32) = floor(root(p * 2^(32k))).
     BC_LINE_LENGTH=0 /usr/bin/bc -q oracle/kiv.bc </dev/null > kiv.out
     05db15f5bb52fd72b5629b48dd6747a0aa74311636af08b72a4153e8cce31559  kiv.out
     awk '$1=="K"{print $4}' kiv.out > sha256-k.dec ; awk '$1=="H"{print $4}' kiv.out > sha256-iv.dec
   Cross-check 1, an independent method: integer Newton roots in Python, and a Decimal computation
   at 120 digits of the fractional parts.
     /usr/bin/python3 oracle/kiv.py kiv.out
     -> PASS bc K/H equal python exact-integer and decimal(120) derivations
   Cross-check 2, the published standard: the hex tables of FIPS 180-4 section 4.2.2 (K) and
   section 5.3.3 (H(0)), extracted by pdftotext.
     pdftotext -layout NIST.FIPS.180-4.pdf fips.txt ; /usr/bin/python3 oracle/fipschk.py fips.txt kiv.out
     -> PASS bc K[0..63] and H(0)[0..7] equal FIPS 180-4 sections 4.2.2 and 5.3.3
   gate.om reads these two files to hash the compiler under test, so its pinned-digest comparison
   exercises them against /usr/bin/sha256sum on every run.

     -> PASS 158 vectors; FIPS 180-2 B.1, B.2, B.3 published digests matched
   The messages are extracted from the NIST texts, never typed: "abc" and the 448-bit message come
   from SHA256.txt, and the 896-bit message (two lines, joined) from SHA512.txt. Their lengths are
   asserted as 3, 56 and 112 bytes. The one-million-'a' message is b"a" * 1000000 (FIPS 180-2 B.3).
   Each digest is produced by /usr/bin/sha256sum and must equal Python hashlib (OpenSSL). The abc,
   448-bit and million-a digests must also equal the values NIST printed in FIPS 180-2 Appendix B
   (B.1, B.2, B.3). The 896-bit and empty-message digests have no published SHA-256 value in these
   documents; for them the two independent implementations agreeing is the only check.
   Rows: 5 FIPS messages; seq:N for N = 0..130, 1000, 4095, 4096, 4097, 8191, 8192, 8193, 65536;
   rep:97:N for N = 55, 56, 63, 64, 65, 4096; and 8 random messages rnd:SEED:N with
   (SEED, N) = (1,3) (2,55) (3,56) (4,64) (5,111) (6,4097) (7,12345) (8,100003).
   Generators, implemented identically in oracle/vectors.py and gate.om:
     seq:N        byte i = i mod 251. The period 251 is prime and not a multiple of 64, so swapped
                  or repeated blocks change the message.
     rep:B:N      N copies of byte B
     rnd:SEED:N   splitmix64: s += 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15; z = s; z = (z ^ z>>30) * 0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9;
                  z = (z ^ z>>27) * 0x94D049BB133111EB; z ^= z>>31; each z gives 8 bytes, little endian.
                  gate.om holds these multipliers in decimal: 11400714819323198485,
                  13787848793156543929, 10723151780598845931. The decimals were converted with
