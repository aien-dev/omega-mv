/tmp/l180-site-dist/research/computing-machinery-and-understanding/paper.tex:\textbf{Assumption A2 (Computable predictive distribution).} The candidate \(M\) must expose its predictive distribution \(p_M\) as a computable function. Where an actual entropy-coded bitstream is not produced, the idealized codelength \(L(D\mid M,G)=-\sum_t \log_2 p_M(x_t\mid x_{<t},G)\) is used, and the profile must record which method was applied (\texttt{codelength\_method}). The profile declares a reference entropy coder, arithmetic coding~\cite{witten1987} or asymmetric numeral systems~\cite{duda2009}, with bits-back coders~\cite{hinton1993,townsend2019} for latent-variable models; ``bits'' in a Turing measurement means bits under the declared coder. Idealized log-loss is realizable to within small bounded amortizable overhead by such coders, and H1 tests the bridge.

/tmp/l180-site-dist/research/computing-machinery-and-understanding/paper.tex:For a predictive distribution \(p_M\) over a finite alphabet and a sequence \(D\) of length \(n\), there exists a code whose length for \(D\) is less than \(-\sum_{t=1}^{n}\log_2 p_M(x_t\mid x_{<t}) + 2\) bits, subject to the declared termination and message-length convention.
/tmp/l180-site-dist/research/computing-machinery-and-understanding/paper.tex:\textit{Proof sketch.} Let \(P_M(D)=\prod_{t=1}^{n}p_M(x_t\mid x_{<t})\) be the probability the model assigns to the complete sequence. Arithmetic coding assigns the whole sequence an interval of width \(P_M(D)\) within \([0,1)\). A binary point identifying that interval requires fewer than \(-\log_2 P_M(D)+2 = -\sum_{t=1}^{n}\log_2 p_M(x_t\mid x_{<t})+2\) bits, under the profile's declared termination and message-length convention (standard Shannon source-coding argument~\cite{shannon1948}; cf.\ textbook treatments of arithmetic coding). \qed

This run, TY-1+TY-2, is a completed pre-EXP instrument result with a fixed model class. It is not EXP-001: the EXP-001 preregistered runs are pending, and the H1 prediction-compression bridge (Section~5.3) remains open. The scoring tool declines to score the held-out seeds a second time: a useful procedural discipline, not proof of blinding. A separate refit check refits both models from the training files and reproduces the receipt exactly, which establishes replayability; non-access to the held-out seeds before the freeze rests on the seed discipline recorded in the profile (seeds 8--10 never seen during model selection; only seed~7 was used), not on the tool's refusal. The complexity charge penalizes stored information that does not earn its cost on held-out observations. Under Lemma~2's assumptions, pure memorization cannot outperform the cheaper baseline. Candidate~C is itself a table-based predictor; its positive held-out gain is consistent with legitimate generalization under the complexity charge, which Lemma~2 does not forbid, subject to the limits stated below: this is an internally replayed result, and independent blinding was not established. All scoring arithmetic uses fixed-point integer micro-bits: 1~bit~=~1{,}000{,}000 micro-bits (\texttt{src/turing/ty\_math.c}). Probabilities are quantized to 16~bits; each symbol's code length \(-\log_2(q/65536)\) is computed with integer fixed-point arithmetic and rounded to the nearest micro-bit. Reported Turing values may therefore carry up to six decimal places: the receipt's exact \texttt{t\_microbits} = 2{,}559{,}679{,}824{,}815 micro-bits (2{,}559{,}679.824815~T\(_{\rm ideal}\)), displayed as +2{,}559{,}679.825~T\(_{\rm ideal}\) rounded to three decimals, not a floating-point approximation. Worst-case rounding is under 6~bits over the whole held-out set. The 6-bit figure is an arithmetic rounding bound on the scoring computation, not a statistical confidence interval; statistical uncertainty for \(\mathcal{T}\) is required future work. The reported baseline is a weak one (a last-outcome table); the profile's \texttt{baseline\_sensitivity} field requires the gain to be reported under two to three reasonable alternative baselines, which is pending (Table~2). Required ablations are an operation-only baseline, an operation-plus-last-outcome baseline, and a longer-history-without-operation baseline; no ablation numbers are reported here (Table~2). Limits stated plainly: $\mathcal{T}$ here is a theoretical code length. No entropy coder was built that produces the smaller file, so this is not yet a \(\mathcal{T}_{\rm real}\) measurement; energy was not measured in this run, so no Turing yield (T/J) is reported. Joining R15 energy measurements to Turing-gain receipts is the next step (Definition~4).
